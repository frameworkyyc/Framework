Gates and posts

Gates that hang straight.

Walk gates, double drive gates up to 12 ft and keypad or magnetic latches. Wide gates get a steel-reinforced frame, because the usual failure is sag, not style.

  • Self-closing hinges for pools and dogs
  • Opener-ready frames for drive gates
  • Black or stainless hardware that will not streak

Posts do the real work.

We set them below the frost line in a concrete collar over gravel, so winter heave does not tilt the line.

  • Utility locates before we dig
  • Closer spacing on windy lots
  • Steel posts where a long run needs them
Finishing details

The small things that make it look expensive.

Most of what separates a good deck or fence from a great one happens at the edges. Each detail has a benefit and a cost; here are both.

  • Picture-frame border

    BenefitHides cut ends and gives the surface a finished edge.

    Trade-offA few more boards and extra labour.

  • Hidden fasteners

    BenefitA smooth, screw-free surface that is kind to bare feet.

    Trade-offSlower to install and a little more material.

  • Fascia wrap

    BenefitCovers the rim joist so the deck looks solid from the yard.

    Trade-offAdds material cost, but it is the detail you see most.

  • Post caps and trim

    BenefitProtect end grain and add character, with lighting options.

    Trade-offMetal or lit caps cost more than plain wood.

  • A stain and seal plan

    BenefitWe tell you when to start and what to use, so wood lasts.

    Trade-offReal maintenance time unless you choose a no-upkeep material.

  • Ledger flashing

    BenefitKeeps water out of the house wall where the deck attaches.

    Trade-offInvisible when it works, which is why it gets skipped. We do not.

  • Slip-resistant stairs

    BenefitGrooved treads and nosing help in wet and icy months.

    Trade-offGrooves collect debris, so they need an occasional sweep.

  • Gate and post hardware

    BenefitStainless or coated hardware that will not rust or streak.

    Trade-offCosts more up front, but you only do it once.

Straight answers

Questions we hear every week.

Based in Calgary, serving the city and surrounding communities.

Calgary · Airdrie · Cochrane · Chestermere · Okotoks · Strathmore · Rocky View County

Cedar or composite?

Cedar costs less up front and looks classic but needs a stain-and-seal routine. Composite costs more and asks very little of you. If you enjoy caring for wood, choose cedar; if you would rather use the deck than maintain it, choose composite. We will price both.

Will a solid fence survive our wind?

Yes, if it is built for it. Solid fences act like sails in strong Chinook gusts, so we set posts deeper, tighten the spacing and often suggest shadow-box, which lets some wind through.

Do I need a permit?

Usually not for a fence under 2 m (6 ft 7 in), but decks over 0.6 m generally do, and rules vary by property, condo and HOA. We check your specific yard and handle what is needed.

How long does a build take?

Most fences go up in 1–3 days once permits and utility locates are done, typically 1–2 weeks from your quote. Decks usually take 1–3 weeks to build, depending on size and the number of levels.

Do you build in winter?

Fences, yes, using ground-thaw equipment. We book deck builds from April to November, when footings and finishes behave.

Can I mix materials?

Absolutely. A composite surface with a cedar privacy screen or an aluminum rail is a popular pairing, and we will show you how they look together before you decide.

What about my neighbour and the property line?

We confirm your property lines and talk through shared fences before we build. If a neighbour shares the cost, we can provide a simple written breakdown.

How do I look after it?

Every project comes with a short care guide: when to clean, when to stain and what to check each spring. Composite and aluminum need little more than a wash.
